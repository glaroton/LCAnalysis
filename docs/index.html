LCAnalysis

Differential and integral calculus on locally convex spaces, formalized in Lean 4 and Mathlib.

API documentation Source on GitHub

This library formalizes the Michal–Bastiani calculus (Keller's C^k_c). It is the usual foundation for infinite-dimensional Lie theory and global analysis, where manifolds and Lie groups are modeled on arbitrary locally convex spaces: Fréchet spaces of smooth functions, spaces of test functions, direct limits. Mathlib's differential calculus (HasFDerivAt, ContDiff) is built on normed spaces, so it does not cover these models.

There is no sorry. The main theorems depend only on Lean's standard axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound; the Verification section explains how to check this.

The setting

Let E, F be real locally convex Hausdorff spaces, U ⊆ E open and f : U → F.

  • Differentiability. f is C^1 if every directional derivative df(x)(h) = lim_{t → 0} t⁻¹ (f (x + t h) - f x) exists and df : U × E → F is continuous. It is C^{k+1} if moreover df is C^k.
  • Tools in place of norm estimates. Hahn–Banach: continuous linear functionals separate points from closed convex sets. Local convexity: closed convex neighborhoods of 0 form a basis.
  • Integrals. Integrals of curves are weak integrals: y = ∫_a^b γ means φ y = ∫_a^b φ (γ t) dt for every continuous linear functional φ. They are unique, but they need not exist. They do exist under mild completeness conditions on F.

Design decisions

  • Michal–Bastiani, not convenient calculus. Convenient calculus [KM97] is the other standard framework for infinite-dimensional analysis. It agrees with Michal–Bastiani calculus on Fréchet spaces but not in general. This library follows the Michal–Bastiani framework of the Lie theory in [Nee06, GN26], which it is meant to support.
  • A recursive definition of C^k. ContDiffMBOn (k + 1) f U means that f is C^1 on U and dMB f is C^k on U ×ˢ univ. This makes induction on k straightforward, for instance in the chain rule and for parameter-dependent integrals. Bastiani's original definition, continuity of all iterated directional derivatives, is HasContIterDerivOn, and the two are proved equivalent.
  • One universe for domains. ContDiffMBOn is defined by recursion on k and quantifies over domains. As a consequence, the domains of C^k maps live in a fixed universe u. Codomains are unrestricted.
  • Existence of integrals is a hypothesis, not an assumption on the space. HasWeakIntegral γ a b y is a relation, so results hold in every locally convex space under explicit existence hypotheses, and completeness enters only through the existence theorems. The function weakIntegral, with junk value 0, is used only where existence is known.
  • Completeness without uniform structures. SeqComplete and MackeyComplete are stated through the topology: a sequence is Cauchy when u n - u m is eventually in every neighborhood of 0. This avoids fixing a uniform structure on a topological vector space. For a uniform additive group, CompleteSpace implies SeqComplete.
  • Mackey completeness through Mackey–Cauchy sequences. MackeyComplete asks every Mackey–Cauchy sequence to converge: u l - u k ∈ r k • B for k ≤ l, with B bounded and r k → 0. This is one of the equivalent characterizations in G–N 1.1.18(e) and [KM97, 2.2, 2.14]. It is the more convenient form for the existence proofs. The definition of G–N 1.1.17(b), that Lipschitz curves have weak integrals, is IntegratesLipschitzCurves, and the two are proved equivalent (mackeyComplete_iff_integratesLipschitzCurves).
  • Mathlib conventions. File layout, naming, docstrings and the linter set follow Mathlib.

What is new here and what is not

The mathematics is not new. Every theorem above is classical and appears in the cited literature. The proofs follow the cited sources, except in these places:

  • All the results on parameter-dependent integrals come from a single induction. Its hypothesis is that every C^m map on U × T has weak integrals in the second variable. The sequentially complete case and the Mackey complete case are then corollaries.
  • Weak integrals are constructed as limits of dyadic Riemann sums. In the continuous case the Cauchy estimate comes from uniform continuity; in the C^1 case it comes from the mean value inclusion.
  • For the converse direction of mackeyComplete_iff_integratesLipschitzCurves, a Mackey–Cauchy sequence is turned into a Lipschitz curve. We pass to a fast subsequence and place its increments on tents supported on the intervals [1 - 2⁻ʲ, 1 - 2⁻ʲ⁻¹]. The weak integral of that curve is the limit of the subsequence.
  • The second part of the fundamental theorem is reduced, through an affine change of variables, to the estimate for integrals of small curves.

The formalization is new. Michal–Bastiani calculus is not in Mathlib, and we are not aware of a formalization of it in another proof assistant.

The library rests on Mathlib. It uses, among others:

  • topological vector spaces, LocallyConvexSpace, von Neumann bounded sets (Bornology.IsVonNBounded), and the Hahn–Banach theorems (SeparatingDual, geometric_hahn_banach_closed_point);
  • the interval integral and the fundamental theorem of calculus for real functions;
  • the Fréchet calculus (HasFDerivAt, ContDiffOn, continuous multilinear maps), against which MBFrechet.lean compares the Michal–Bastiani notions.

How this library was written

Gabriel Larotonda set the mathematical program and chose the framework and the references. He also fixed the design constraints (no sorry, Michal–Bastiani rather than convenient calculus, Mathlib conventions) and reviewed the results. The Lean proofs were written by the AI assistant Claude (Anthropic), working under his direction.

Correctness does not depend on trusting either of them:

  • Lean's kernel checks every proof.
  • The axiom audit below can be reproduced by anyone.
  • The definitions and theorem statements are kept close to the cited literature, so they can be compared with it directly.

Verification

Requirements: elan. The Lean toolchain (lean-toolchain) and the Mathlib version (lakefile.toml) are pinned.

lake exe cache get                  # download compiled Mathlib
lake build                          # build the library
lake env lean scripts/Axioms.lean   # print the axioms used by the main theorems

For every theorem, the last command should report the axioms [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]. The continuous integration workflow runs the same check, and it fails if any other axiom appears, including sorryAx.
